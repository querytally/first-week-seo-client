How to Do a Content Gap Analysis: Four Kinds of Gap

To do a content gap analysis, list the topics your buyers search for, set each one against your own pages and your competitors' pages, and sort every topic into one of four kinds of gap. Each kind has its own fix: write, rewrite, write first, or protect.

The five steps

  1. Name the rivals. Three competitors your buyers actually compare you with. Wrong names give a wrong list.
  2. List the topics. The questions and needs your buyers search for, each with its monthly searches.
  3. Match the pages. For each topic, the page of yours that answers it, and each rival's page, as links.
  4. Sort into the four kinds below.
  5. Turn the rows into a plan: one page per topic, with an owner and a date.

The four kinds of gap

The example throughout: an accounting firm that works only with small restaurants and runs a blog for owners.

1. Catch-up: they have a page, you have none

Two rivals have a guide to payroll taxes for restaurant staff with tipped wages. The firm has nothing on it. Fix: write the page, and make it better than theirs, not a copy of their outline.

2. Thin: you have a page, theirs answers better

The firm has a 150-word post on food cost percentage. A rival has a worked example with a menu, costs and the percentage for each dish. Fix: rewrite the existing page. It keeps its address and any links it already has.

3. Open: people search, nobody has a page

Owners ask how to split pooled tips between front and back of house. No rival has a page that answers it. Fix: write it first. It is the one kind where you meet the demand alone.

4. Ahead: you have a page, they do not

The firm has a plain guide to reading a restaurant's monthly profit and loss statement. No rival covers it. Fix: protect it. Keep it current and link to it from the firm's other posts.

Content gap or keyword gap?

keyword gap content gap
the unit one phrase one topic, one page
a row looks like "restaurant bookkeeping" restaurant bookkeeping, with "bookkeeping for restaurants" and "restaurant bookkeeping services" folded in
what it tells you which phrases a rival answers and you do not which pages to write or rewrite
the risk three rows that tempt you into three pages none, if the folding is done right

One content gap can hold many keyword gaps. The page is what you build, so the page is the row that matters.

Turning gaps into pages

  • Sort by volume, then by buyer. Start with the biggest monthly searches, but move a topic up when the person searching is close to hiring. "Restaurant accountant near me" beats a bigger search from students.
  • Open the rival's page before you decide. A catch-up gap against a weak page is a quick win. Against a strong page, you need a better angle.
  • One topic, one page. If a new topic shares its intent with a page you already have, improve that page instead.
  • Give each page an owner and a date. A gap list with no owner is a document. With owners, it is a plan.
  • Link new pages to the ones that sell. A guide that ranks but never points to your service page wins readers, not clients.

What a good list looks like

A useful content gap list names each topic, gives its US monthly searches, links the competitor's page, and links yours or says none. If a row says none, you should be able to open the rival's site and see where the page would be.

Quick checklist

  • [ ] Three named rivals your buyers compare you with
  • [ ] Similar phrases folded into one topic per row
  • [ ] A link for every rival page, and for every page of yours
  • [ ] Each row marked catch-up, thin, open or ahead
  • [ ] Topics that bring readers who never buy taken off the list
  • [ ] An owner and a date on every page you plan
