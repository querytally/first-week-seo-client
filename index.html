The first week after an SEO client signs: what to put in front of them

Published by Querytally. We sell research to SEO agencies, so we have an interest in the subject.

The contract is signed and the client is happy. Then comes a quiet stretch. The kick-off call is booked, access requests go back and forth, and the research that month one depends on has not started yet. From the client's side, nothing visible happens.

That quiet stretch is where many agency relationships start to cool. The owner who signed with energy now waits. Their first impression of the work is a request for logins.

What the client needs to see first

An owner who just signed wants proof that they chose well. A ranking report cannot give it yet. A site score does not mean much to most owners. What does land is something about their own market that they did not know.

The strongest version of that is short: a list of pages their competitors have and they lack. Each row names a topic people search for, how many US searches it draws a month, and which competitor has a page for it, with the link. The owner can click any row and see the rival's page. Nothing has to be taken on trust.

That one page does three jobs at once.

  • It shows the client you looked at their business, not a template.
  • It gives month one a visible starting point.
  • It turns the first meeting from "what do you want from SEO?" into "which of these do we build first?"

Order the list with the client, not for them

Bring the list to the kick-off and sort it together. Some rows will be services they offer and want more of. Some will be services they dropped years ago. Some will make them say "we do that, but nobody knows". Only the owner can tell those apart.

Each row also carries its intent: buy, compare or learn. Rows where people want to buy usually come first, because they are closest to revenue. Learning rows can wait for month two or three, when the buying pages are live and need support.

By the end of the call you have a short, ordered plan the client helped write. That plan is much harder to second-guess in month four.

When there is nothing to show

Sometimes a client's site already covers what its rivals cover. That is useful news too. Say it plainly. It moves the conversation to the pages they have and how good they are, and it tells the owner you report what is there, not what sells the next invoice.

Keep the research out of the kick-off's way

The first week is already full: access, setup, the reporting calendar. When the research has to squeeze in beside all that, it is usually what slips, and the client never sees the page that would have reassured them. Have it ready before the kick-off, not after.

The page you hand over should look like yours. If it carries no other company's logo, it sits in your onboarding pack as your own work, next to your welcome note and your reporting dates.

What the page should not promise

Keep it to rows a person can check. No rankings, no traffic forecasts, no claim to list every search in the market. A short list of real gaps, each with a link and a number, does more for trust than a long one full of guesses.
