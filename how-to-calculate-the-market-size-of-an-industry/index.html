How to Calculate the Market Size of an Industry

To calculate the market size of an industry, multiply the number of buyers by what each one spends in a year (bottom-up), then check it against a larger published total cut down to your part of it (top-down). If the two answers land close together, the size holds; if they land far apart, one input is wrong.

This page works both directions on one industry in a single table, then sizes the same market a third way: by how many people ask for it each month.

The industry in the example: freight factoring

Freight factoring is a finance service for trucking companies. A carrier sells its unpaid invoices to a factoring company and gets cash within a day or two instead of waiting 30 to 60 days. The factoring company keeps a fee, a few percent of each invoice.

The money figures below are round and only show how the sums work. They are not a forecast.

Top-down and bottom-up, side by side

Step Top-down Bottom-up
Start from A published total for all US factoring fees: 5 billion dollars a year US trucking carriers with 1 to 10 trucks: 300,000
Narrow to the industry Share of factoring that is freight: 30% Share of those carriers that factor: 30%
Result so far 1.5 billion dollars 90,000 carriers
Multiply by spend Fees per carrier a year: 15,000 dollars
Market size 1.5 billion dollars a year 1.35 billion dollars a year
Gap between the two 10%

A 10% gap is close enough to trust the range: roughly 1.35 to 1.5 billion dollars a year in fees. Had the bottom-up answer come out at 400 million, something would be off, most likely the share of carriers that factor or the fee per carrier.

Which direction to trust for what

Use Better direction Why
A pitch deck or a first look Top-down Quick, and investors expect the big number
A business plan or a budget Bottom-up Built from buyers you can name and count
Checking someone else's estimate Both A single direction hides a wrong input
A new industry with no published total Bottom-up There is nothing to cut down

The three layers every estimate should name

  • Total market: every buyer who could use the service. In the example, the 1.35 to 1.5 billion dollars.
  • Serviceable market: the part your offer can reach. A factoring firm that only serves carriers in five states might reach a fifth of it.
  • Obtainable share: what you can win in the next year or two. This is the number to plan hiring and spending around.

An estimate that does not say which layer it is cannot be compared with another one.

The third view: the same market in searches

Money says how much is spent. It does not say how many buyers are asking right now, or what they ask for. Sizing the market in monthly searches answers that.

For freight factoring, the whole US market comes to about 309,080 searches a month. That figure is a count of people asking, across all the needs in the market: rates, contracts, how fast payment comes, whether a carrier with a new authority qualifies, and many more.

Not all of it is buyer demand. Of 1,569 searches that looked relevant, 272 came from the wrong audience. Together they draw about 24,460 searches a month. Take those out and about 284,620 searches a month remain from people who could buy.

Reading the three views together

View Freight factoring What it tells you
Top-down About 1.5 billion dollars a year How large the industry is
Bottom-up About 1.35 billion dollars a year Whether the buyers add up to that
Searches About 284,620 a month from possible buyers How many are asking now, and for what

The money views tell you whether an industry is worth entering. The search view tells you where to start: which needs draw the most people and which ones no one serves well yet.

A short checklist before quoting a size

  • Does it say which layer it is: total, serviceable or obtainable?
  • Does it name a country and a period?
  • Was it worked from both directions, and do they land close?
  • Is the wrong audience taken out of any search count?
  • Can a reader follow every multiplication on the page?
